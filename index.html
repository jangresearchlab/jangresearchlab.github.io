Welcome to the Jang Lab

Advancing precision psychiatry through real-world human brain research

The Jang Laboratory aims to understand how the brain learns, makes decisions, and generates emotions — and how these processes shape behavior and mental health. We integrate immersive virtual reality (VR), mobile intracranial EEG, wearable technologies, motion tracking, and computational modeling to study how neural circuits support behavior in naturalistic settings. Our goal is to connect brain activity to real-world experience in ways that deepen our understanding of learning and decision-making, reveal how these processes differ in psychiatric disorders, and guide the development of more targeted, circuit-based interventions.

The Jang Lab is part of the UCLA Department of Psychiatry and Biobehavioral Sciences and the Jane and Terry Semel Institute for Neuroscience and Human Behavior, Division of Neuromodulation.

Research

Virtual Reality (VR) Fear Conditioning

This project uses immersive virtual reality combined with intracranial EEG recordings to study the neural mechanisms underlying the formation and extinction of fear responses in realistic environments. Participants move through richly detailed virtual settings where cues become associated with threat or safety, allowing us to measure how fear memories form, generalize, and change over time. By synchronizing neural activity with behavior, movement, and physiology, we can directly observe how neural circuits encode fear and regulate responses to threat. Ultimately, this work aims to identify neural signatures of fear and anxiety that could guide more precise, circuit-based interventions.

Collaborator(s): Nanthia Suthana (Duke)

Closed-Loop Neurostimulation for Treatment-Resistant PTSD

This project investigates whether brain-responsive neurostimulation can alleviate severe, treatment-resistant PTSD by detecting neural signatures of distress and delivering targeted stimulation in real time. By combining implanted sensing devices with behavioral and physiological measures, we aim to identify when pathological fear circuits become active and intervene at the moment they drive symptoms. This closed-loop approach offers a path toward more precise, personalized neuromodulation for individuals who have not benefited from existing therapies.

Collaborator(s): Nanthia Suthana (Duke), Jean-Philippe Langevin (UCLA/VA)

Cognitive Maps and Flexible Learning

This project studies how the human brain builds and updates internal cognitive maps that support flexible learning and generalization across environments. Using immersive virtual reality and intracranial EEG, we examine how neural representations of the environment are updated when the underlying structure of a task changes. By linking these neural dynamics to behavior, this work aims to reveal how the brain infers abstract relationships and enables the transfer of knowledge across contexts to guide decision-making.

Collaborator(s): Matt Nassar (Brown)

People

Principal Investigator

Anthony I Jang, MD
Assistant Professor-In-Residence, Department of Psychiatry and Biobehavioral Sciences

Anthony received his undergraduate degree in neuroscience at Brown University, where he worked with Michael Frank and Matthew Nassar to study how reward prediction errors shape memory. He then completed post-baccalaureate research at the NIH with Kareem Zaghloul, using intracranial EEG and microelectrode recordings to investigate the neural basis of memory, attention, and decision-making. He earned his MD through the Harvard–MIT Program in Health Sciences and Technology (HST), where he trained with Jan Drugowitsch to develop theory-driven models of human choice and eye-movement behavior. After graduating in 2021, he moved to the West Coast to complete his psychiatry residency at UCLA. As a Research Track resident, he worked with Nanthia Suthana, integrating immersive virtual reality, mobile intracranial EEG, and wearable technologies to study fear learning and anxiety. Anthony joined UCLA in Fall 2025 as an Assistant Professor-in-Residence in the Department of Psychiatry and Biobehavioral Sciences. Anthony is interested in integrating intracranial electrophysiology, virtual reality, and computational approaches to understand how the human brain learns, makes decisions, and generates emotion in naturalistic settings. Outside the lab, Anthony enjoys playing tennis, spending time with his wife and two cats, and F1 sim racing.
